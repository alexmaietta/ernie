Detailed Description

Spectral quantities for Ernie's spectral rendering: wavelengths in nanometres, spectral irradiance in W m^-2 nm^-1 and spectral radiance in W m^-2 sr^-1 nm^-1.

The Sun's light above any atmosphere.

Light scattering by molecules and particles, for participating media such as atmospheres: Rayleigh scattering by gases, Mie scattering by spheres and particle size distributions, and tabulated absorption cross sections.

A planet's atmosphere as constituents (gases, aerosols, absorbers) with physical optical properties and vertical density profiles, and its reduction to a camera's spectral bands.

Deterministic random streams shared by every render backend.

How every render delegate reads a Scene: which prims a render uses, its render products, and the camera, lights, materials, meshes and instances as plain data, so backends differ only in how they draw.

Rays of a lens in the camera's frame, and the cube faces a wide camera's views are rendered on, for fields the single pinhole raster image cannot take (its tangent grows without bound towards 90 degrees off axis).

Helpers for authoring and reading the schemas in tokens.h.

Scenes described in JSON, the format of Python's ernie.load_scene:

The view reads and writes the scene's attributes by their USD names, so it changes nothing about storage or change tracking; unset attributes read as the declared fallback, the one place Ernie keeps it.

A schema's header declares its attributes with an X-macro list and its class with ERNIE_SCHEMA_BODY; its source defines the registry entry with ERNIE_DEFINE_SCHEMA, and prim_type.cpp lists it in the registry. See light_schemas.h.

References: * Blender, Cycles' node types and sockets (NODE_DEFINE, SOCKET_*, NODE_SOCKET_API), https://projects.blender.org/blender/cycles/src/branch/main/src/graph/node_type.h * Pixar, UsdLux and generated schema classes, https://openusd.org/release/api/usd_lux_page_front.html

  • The top level maps prim paths (absolute, or relative to the root) to prims.
  • A prim is an object: "type" names its type (Scene::Define; registered or not). Without one, a new prim is untyped and an existing prim keeps its type and attributes. Keys whose values are objects are its children, by name; the other keys are its attributes, by the Python name of one its type declares (AttributeDef::pyName, "cell_size") or by USD name ("inputs:intensity").
  • translate (3 numbers), rotate (3 Euler angles in degrees), rotation_order ("XYZ", ..., "ZYX"), orient (a quaternion as [w, x, y, z], the real part first as USD writes one and as Python's Quat(w, x, y, z) takes it) and scale (one number or 3) set its transform ops (SetXform); transform sets xformOp:transform to a 4 x 4 matrix given as 4 rows of 4 numbers (math convention: column vectors, translation in the last column).
  • Values convert as Python's do: booleans, integers (32-bit), other numbers as doubles, strings, arrays of strings, arrays of 2 numbers (ivec2 when both are integers), 3 or 4 as vectors, other lengths as float arrays, and 4 x 4 nested arrays as matrices; nested arrays other than 4 x 4 are a float32 tensor.
  • Those are the rules for undeclared attributes only. A declared one, or a transform op (FindXformOp: xformOp:orient:<suffix> too), is read as its kind: a tensor (points, heights) as a CPU tensor, int32 for one of whole numbers (AttributeMeta::integer: indices, counts) and float32 for any other, whatever the numbers look like, written with Scene::Write; an AttributeMeta::array attribute's array of values, nested deeper than one value (a per-vertex primvars:displayColor, N rows of 3), as a float32 tensor; a Vec4's 4 numbers as doubles, a Quat's as [w, x, y, z] and a Matrix as 4 rows; then converted to its type (AttributeDef::Coerce: a string to a relationship target, say), a AttributeMeta::lenient attribute keeping a value its type cannot convert. Values are checked as Scene::Set checks them (Validate).

SaveScene writes the format back, so that LoadScene of its text into an empty scene rebuilds the scene: every prim but the viewer's (under tokens::kSessionRoot), roots by path at the top level and the others nested as children by name, "type" first when typed, then the attributes by USD name in name order (transform ops as xformOp:* and xformOpOrder attributes, not the translate-style keys), then the children in path order. Values are written as LoadScene reads them back: Paths and lists of them as strings, matrices as 4 rows, quaternions as [w, x, y, z], floats in their shortest form that reads back as the same float, and tensors as arrays nested by their shape. Limits: * large tensors make large files (a number per element, a line per row), and NaN or infinite numbers cannot be written (JSON has none); * declared attributes come back as their declared types; undeclared ones as LoadScene reads the JSON: Paths as strings, 2 to 4 numbers as vectors (a float array or a rank-1 tensor of that length among them, and a dvec4 or a quaternion off xformOp:orient as a float vec4), other rank-1 arrays as float arrays, nested arrays as float32 tensors (any element type), and a 4 x 4 tensor as a matrix; * declared tensors come back int32 (AttributeMeta::integer) or float32 whatever their element type; an empty tensor comes back without its shape and a rank-0 one with shape [1]; * an empty list of strings or paths comes back as an empty float array (declared relationship lists convert it back), and unset (Value::Empty) values are left out; * overlays (Scene::GetOverlay) are not prims and are not written; * GPU tensors are not copied to the host: SaveScene throws for them.

The readers accept any numeric type an author might use for an attribute (an int, float or double number; a vec or dvec vector), since bindings such as Python's produce doubles.

The camera's frame is USD's: x right, y up, looking down -z. A cube face is a square pinhole view of 90 degrees (plus a margin) along one of the camera's axes; the sensor follows each pixel's lens ray to the face it leaves through. kernel/film/cube_faces.slang holds the same conventions for the sensor kernel.

References: * N. Greene, "Environment Mapping and Other Applications of World Projections", IEEE Computer Graphics and Applications 6(11):21-29, 1986 (cube maps). * Khronos, "Vulkan Specification", 16.5.4 "Cube Map Face Selection" (selection by the largest-magnitude component).

Every random decision a renderer makes (sensor noise, and the samples of ray and path tracers) derives from three numbers: a seed the scene authors, a stream naming what is rendered (a render product, StreamOf its path) and the frame number. The same scene, settings and frame then render the same image on any machine, in any order, whatever else renders alongside.

Reference: M. Jarzynski and M. Olano, "Hash Functions for GPU Rendering", Journal of Computer Graphics Techniques 9(3):21-38, 2020 (pcg3d).

Altitudes and radii are in metres, number densities in m^-3, cross sections in m^2 and coefficients in m^-1.

Wavelengths are in nanometres in vacuum, radii in metres and cross sections in m^2. Phase functions are in sr^-1, normalised to integrate to 1 over the sphere, and take the cosine of the scattering angle (1 is forward).

function StandardSensorFilters

The standard library of sensor filters, written in Slang like custom ones and registered with every renderer, for a SensorFilter prim's ernie:filter:type :

std::vector< SensorFilterModel > ernie::StandardSensorFilters ()
  • psf (spatial, spectrum): the lens's point-spread, diffraction for the camera's f-number per band (a Gaussian fit to the Airy disc) and a Gaussian blur in pixels; before qe.
  • defocus (spatial, spectrum): thin-lens depth of field from the camera's fStop and focusDistance; before qe.
  • bayer (pixel, electrons): a Bayer colour filter mosaic, pattern 0 RGGB, 1 BGGR, 2 GRBG, 3 GBRG; best between noise and adc.
  • demosaic (spatial, digital): interpolates the mosaic back, method 0 bilinear or 1 Malvar-He-Cutler; after adc. AOV mosaic: the mosaic it read.
  • blooming (spatial, electrons): charge beyond the full well spilling along columns, antiBlooming the fraction drained; after qe. AOV overflow: the charge beyond the well.
  • whiteBalance (pixel, rgb): red, green and blue gains; after cie.
  • colorMatrix (pixel, rgb): a 3 x 3 colour correction matrix m00..m22; after cie.
  • compand (pixel, digital): square-root companding from inputBits to outputBits; after adc.
  • toneCurve (pixel, display): each channel through curve (33 samples over 0 to 1); after exposure.
  • spectralTransmission (band): each band times curve (48 samples, 360 to 830 nm), a measured filter's transmission; before qe and cie.
  • glare (spatial, rgb; pyramid): a wide power-law halo of scattered light around bright sources in the image; after cie.
  • flare (spatial, electrons; pyramid): lens flare ghosts, the image's bright sources as defocused aperture shapes on the line through the principal point, each at its own scale, size and coating tint; after qe, before noise.
  • strayLight (band): the sun's light from outside the field as a veil over the image, its irradiance times the optics' point source transmittance at its angle off the axis (from the frame's sun, which the image lacks); before qe and cie.
  • lag (pixel, electrons; temporal): a fraction of each frame's charge read out with the next.
  • temporalAverage (pixel, rgb; temporal): an exponential moving average, alpha the new frame's weight. Each file's header cites its references.

Detailed Description

Coordinates are the body-fixed frame: z towards the north pole and x towards longitude 0, in the same units as the radius. Each face is a grid of FaceCells() x FaceCells() quads whose FaceCells() + 1 squared vertices carry heights above the radius; vertex (col, row) of face f lies in direction FaceDirection(f, s, t) with s = 2 col / FaceCells() - 1 and t = 2 row / FaceCells() - 1. Vertices on an edge or corner of the cube appear on every face sharing it and must carry the same height there.

Faces 0 to 3 straddle the equator, centred on longitudes 0, 90, 180 and 270 degrees east, with columns running east and rows running south. Face 4 is centred on the north pole and face 5 on the south pole. On every face, column x row points into the body, so all faces wind the same way. Adjacent faces may run their grids in opposite directions along a shared edge.

The projection is equiangular (Ronchi et al. 1996): s and t map to the face plane through tan(pi s / 4), so cells span nearly equal angles and areas differ by at most a factor of about 1.4 over a face, against 5.2 for a plain gnomonic cube. Grid lines are great circles.

Select runs Strugar's algorithm on all six trees, using distances to each node's CubeSphereRegion. Neighbouring patches, across faces too, differ by at most one level when the first range is at least MinFirstRange. Odd vertices of a patch morph in 3D towards the midpoint of their two neighbours on the next coarser level: along the patch's edges these are the edge's own neighbours, and for vertices odd in both directions those on the quad diagonal from (1, 0) to (0, 1). Unlike the flat terrain's slide towards lower indices, this does not depend on which way a face's grid runs, so edges between faces match, and a fully morphed patch is exactly the coarser mesh.

Refinement (SetRefinement) adds levels finer than the data: with k extra levels each face is a grid of FineCells() = 2^k FaceCells() quads, the data's vertices every 2^k of its lines and heights between them interpolated, so the surface keeps its shape (the sphere's curvature and the data's heights) down to cells 2^k times smaller than the data's near the eye. Levels then count from the finest, a data level l being level l + k, and patches' origins are on the fine grid. A refined node's heights are its data leaf's.

The body may be an ellipsoid (SetRadii), triaxial or not: the cube's grid stays the same in direction, a height of 0 lies on the ellipsoid along the vertex's direction (BasePoint), and heights rise along the ellipsoid's geodetic normal there (SurfaceNormal). Node regions widen by bounds on how much the surface radius and the normal's tilt can vary, so selection stays conservative.

Positions on a planet need more precision than single floats. Selection works in doubles, and its frustum is relative to the eye: build it from a view-projection matrix whose view has the eye at the origin and the body frame's axes.

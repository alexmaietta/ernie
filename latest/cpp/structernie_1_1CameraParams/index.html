Detailed Description

The pose transform maps camera to world coordinates; as in USD the camera looks down its -z axis with +y up. Interactive controls such as OrbitController write it.

Focal length and apertures are read in millimetres. The image fits the vertical aperture: pixels are square with a pitch of verticalAperture / height, and the horizontal aperture follows from the image's aspect. Aperture offsets shift the principal point.

An orthographic camera follows UsdGeomCamera (and GfCamera): its apertures and their offsets are in tenths of a scene unit, the size of the view in the scene, and it has no focal length; every ray runs along the view direction. Its optics are telecentric, without fall-off off axis or depth of field, and its pixels have the pitch orthographicPixelPitchUm on the sensor. Its lens has no distortion. Distances to the camera are from its image plane. Terrain levels of detail still follow the distance from the camera's position, so place it near what it sees.

Radiometry follows the camera equation: a scene radiance L reaches the sensor as irradiance E = L pi t cos^4(theta) / (4 N^2) for an object far away, with transmission t, f-number N and field angle theta; a pixel collects E times its area times the exposure time, converted to photons and electrons per band. The photographic outputs instead scale luminance by USD's linear exposure scale; with a one-channel (monochrome) sensor they are grey, weighting the bands by that channel's quantum efficiency, scaled so a flat spectrum keeps its luminance.

References: * B. K. P. Horn, "Robot Vision", MIT Press, 1986 (the image irradiance equation E = L (pi / 4) (d / f)^2 cos^4(alpha), with N = f / d). * Pixar, "UsdGeomCamera", https://openusd.org/release/api/class_usd_geom_camera.html

variable pushbroom

A pushbroom (line scan) camera ( ernie:camera:scan "pushbroom"): one detector line along the image's x axis,lineOffsetMm off the principal point along y on the focal plane, read out once per image row as the camera moves.

bool ernie::CameraParams::pushbroom;

Row r (from the top) is exposed at the pose (r + 0.5) / height of the way from the last frame's pose to this frame's, so a frame is one strip of the scan. Its lens may distort radially (k1 to k3, with r^2 from the line's distance off the axis too), moving pixels along the line only; it has no tangential distortion, shutter interval or rolling readout of its own.

Reference: R. Gupta and R. I. Hartley, "Linear Pushbroom Cameras", IEEE TPAMI 19(9):963-975, *

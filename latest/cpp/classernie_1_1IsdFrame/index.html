Detailed Description

Unlike the line scanner, the camera looks along (x, y, f) in the sensor's frame, and a ray meets the ellipsoid at its first intersection along it. A rolling shutter's jitter (jitter), as usgscsm models it, moves each image line's pixels by an offset polynomial in the line's exposure time, the pose left at the centre time: image coordinates less the jitter are the unjittered camera's.

References: * usgscsm's UsgsAstroFrameSensorModel (imageToGround, groundToImage, losEllipsoidIntersect, constructStateFromIsd), and its src/Utilities.cpp (removeJitter, addJitter).

The jitter is a shift of each line's pixels, which Ernie's camera cannot take exactly: a rolling shutter (rollingShutter) moves the pose linearly across the rows, while usgscsm keeps the pose and moves the pixels by a polynomial in time. So its midrange over the lines moves the principal point and the rest, half its range, is added to distortionResidualPixels.

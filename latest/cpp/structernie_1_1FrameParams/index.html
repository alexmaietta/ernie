variable origin

The world position the GPU's coordinates are relative to (camera-relative rendering, a floating origin): passes see world points as point - origin , so float precision follows the camera instead of the world's origin, as planet-scale scenes need.

glm::dvec3 ernie::FrameParams::origin;

Chosen near the camera and moved only when the camera gets far from it (RasterDelegate), so what is expressed relative to it is recomputed rarely.

References: * C. Thorne, "Using a Floating Origin to Improve Fidelity and Performance of Large, Distributed Virtual Worlds", CW 2005. https://doi.org/10.1109/CW.2005.94 * P. Cozzi and K. Ring, "3D Engine Design for Virtual Globes", CRC Press, 2011, chapter 5 (rendering relative to center and to eye).

function Faces

The frame as the faces cube faces of a wide camera see it (wide_view.h ): a view per view and face, viewv 's facef atv * faces + f , posed at the view's pose turned onto the face (CubeFaceAxes , its previous pose too) and seeing the view's world, andcamera a centred pinhole of the same image whose square of 90 degrees plus a margin (CubeFaceExtent ) spans the image's shorter side.

FrameParams ernie::FrameParams::Faces (
    int faces
) const

The raster passes draw these; the sensor follows the original camera's lens rays onto them. A copy for one face. With the poses on the GPU, poses are their estimates, and the GPU turns the poses themselves (views.slang).

Exception:

  • std::invalid_argument for faces other than 1, kCubeFaces and kAllCubeFaces.
